AutoMapperLite vs. AutoMapper vs. Mapster

A feature-level comparison to help you choose the right tool. No unverified performance claims about other projects.

Feature comparison

CapabilityAutoMapperLiteAutoMapperMapster
LicenseMIT, freeCommercial license required above revenue thresholdMIT, free
Runtime mapping strategyReflection + cached metadataCompiled expressionsCompiled expressions / source generators
Dependency footprintOne small package (Microsoft.Extensions.DependencyInjection.Abstractions)Larger, more moving partsSmall
LINQ IQueryable projection (ProjectTo)NoYesYes
Convention-based auto-mappingSame-name, same-type propertiesExtensive convention engineExtensive convention engine
ForMember / custom member mappingYesYesYes
Deep nested destination pathsYes (ForPath)YesYes
Collection mappingYes, incl. nested List<T> propertiesYesYes
Dependency injection integrationBuilt in (AddAutoMapperLite)Via separate packageVia separate package
.NET targetsnet6.0 – net10.0Broad, actively maintainedBroad, actively maintained

When to choose which

Choose AutoMapperLite

You want straightforward DTO/entity mapping with a minimal dependency footprint and no commercial licensing considerations, and don't need LINQ projection.

Choose AutoMapper

You need ProjectTo-style LINQ projection, a large convention/configuration surface, or already depend on its ecosystem.

Choose Mapster

You want compiled-expression or source-generator performance with a broad feature set and are comfortable benchmarking it against your specific workload.

See Performance for AutoMapperLite's own measured numbers, and the note there on why we don't publish head-to-head speed claims against these projects.
